# Bounded formula contracts for specialist allowance tools

Research snapshot: 7 October 2026. These are implementation contracts for **illustrative schedules**, not automated determinations that a deduction is legally available. Use official SARS guidance as the formula source, apply the taxpayer's actual financial year, retain assumptions with results, and route doubtful eligibility to a registered practitioner. SARS interpretation notes are detailed and fact-sensitive; do not assert that a checkbox verifies them.

## Section 12C manufacturing wear and tear

Primary source: [SARS Tax Guide for Small Businesses (2025/2026), manufacturing assets section](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Ops/Guides/Legal-Pub-Guide-Gen09-Tax-Guide-for-Small-Businesses.pdf), pp. 28–29. The guide describes qualifying assets and two schedules: new/unused machinery or plant acquired on or after 1 March 2002 and brought into direct manufacturing use: **40% of eligible cost in first assessment year, then 20% in each of three succeeding years**; other qualifying assets: **20% in each of five years**. Eligible cost is no greater than the lesser of actual cost and arm's-length cash price. The same guide gives qualifying research-and-development machinery a **different 50/30/20** schedule. A narrow manufacturing tool should explicitly exclude R&D, hotelkeepers, agricultural co-operatives, lessors and other cases rather than silently apply the wrong schedule.

Suggested inputs: cost, arm's-length cash price (or confirmation they are equal), new/unused versus used, first-use date, financial year-end and confirmation of direct manufacturing use/ownership. Schedule over assessment years starting with the year containing first use. Example for qualifying R100,000 new machinery: R40,000, R20,000, R20,000, R20,000. Qualifying used machinery: R20,000 five times. A delivery truck transporting finished goods should **not** pass a direct-manufacturing confirmation merely because a manufacturer owns it; [SARS's SBC interpretation note](https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-intr-in-2012-09-small-business-corporations/) illustrates the direct/indirect distinction. Do not stack 12C and 12E allowances on the same asset.

Part-year 12C treatment must not be borrowed from general 11(e): a [SARS draft interpretation note](https://www.sars.gov.za/legal-lprep-draft-2025-40-draft-in-the-meaning-of-deemed-to-be-one-and-the-same-person-interaction-between-ss-12c-and-44-18-november-2025/) describes full first-year allowance on first use even for a part year. Because that document is draft, show a schedule by assessment year with no invented day-based proration, and verify current enacted wording and final guidance before tax-return use.

## Section 12E SBC accelerated wear and tear

Primary source: [SARS Interpretation Note 9, Issue 7](https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-intr-in-2012-09-small-business-corporations/), especially sections 4.2.1–4.2.4. Qualifying SBC plant/machinery **directly** used in manufacturing may be deducted **100% in the first assessment year brought into use**. Other qualifying tangible depreciable assets may elect per asset either section 11(e) or the accelerated **50%, 30%, 20% of cost** in the first, first succeeding and second succeeding assessment years. The latter is **not prorated for part-year use**, but the asset must be used in trade during each relevant year. The note says SBC status is needed both in the acquisition and first-use years; grants and small-business funding can limit total deductions. A mere business-size input does not establish SBC eligibility.

Suggested bounded inputs: asset cost/arm's-length cost, asset class (direct manufacturing versus other eligible tangible), acquisition date, first-use date, financial year-end, confirmation of SBC status at both dates, and optional grant/funding flag. If grants, disposals, movement costs or nonstandard years are present, stop short of an unqualified 100% schedule and explain why practitioner review is needed. A R100,000 qualifying manufacturing mixer yields R100,000 first year; a R100,000 qualifying delivery vehicle under the 50/30/20 election yields R50,000/R30,000/R20,000, not R100,000 first year. A R6,000 standalone item might favour an 11(e) small-item election; do not automatically choose 50/30/20. An asset acquired while SBC but brought into use after losing SBC status is a counterexample to automatic 12E eligibility. No double deduction with 12C or 11(e).

## Section 11(f) lease premium

Primary source: [SARS Interpretation Note 109, Issue 2](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Legal/Notes/Legal-IntR-IN-109-Lease-premiums.pdf), sections 3.2.1–3.2.8. For an eligible premium **actually paid** by a lessee for use/occupation of qualifying income-producing property, the usual annual allowance is the **greater of paid premium / years of entitlement and paid premium / 25**, which caps the spread at 25 years. The entitlement period may include renewal rights; the exact period is fact-dependent. Prorate a year in which the qualifying property is used for only part of that assessment year. On early termination or ending income-producing use, the unclaimed balance is **not** automatically deductible under 11(f). The note has a different rule for specified know-how premiums, which a simple property-premium tool must explicitly exclude.

Suggested inputs: premium actually paid, commencement/end and relevant renewal rights, year-end, actual income-producing use dates and an explicit confirmation that this is a lease premium rather than ordinary rent or know-how. In a simple five-year eligible lease, R100,000 gives a full-year R20,000 annual run rate, prorated at the endpoints. In a 30-year eligible lease, the run rate is R4,000, not R3,333, because of the 25-year cap. Do not use the full R20,000 for only half a year of qualifying use, and do not award an immediate R80,000 remainder on early termination.

## Section 11(g) leasehold improvements

Primary source: [SARS Interpretation Note 110, Issue 2](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Legal/Notes/Legal-IntR-IN-110-Leasehold-improvements.pdf), sections 3.2.1–3.2.8. The lessee needs an **enforceable obligation in the lease agreement** to improve income-producing land/buildings. Voluntary improvements do not qualify under 11(g). Total deductible cost is limited to actual expenditure and the amount stipulated in the agreement; if none is stipulated, it is limited by the objectively fair and reasonable value. Spread that base over the remaining right-of-use period **from completion**, capped at 25 years. Prorate the completion year and any partial year of qualifying use. Renewal/indefinite periods require a fact-specific probable duration. Unlike 11(f), section 11(g) provides an additional unclaimed-balance deduction on qualifying early lease termination, which needs separate logic and review.

Suggested inputs: actual cost, contractual amount or a documented fair-value input, dates of lease/renewal/completion, financial year-end and confirmations of enforceable obligation and income use. R550,000 actually spent against R600,000 stipulated gives a R550,000 base. R700,000 spent against R600,000 stipulated gives R600,000. If completed on the last day of the assessment year, there is no meaningful first-year allowance in the [SARS example](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Legal/Notes/Legal-IntR-IN-110-Leasehold-improvements.pdf); the run begins in the next assessment year over the remaining term. If the improvement was voluntary or completion is after lease expiry, show no 11(g) allowance. An early termination must not be handled like 11(f).

## General section 11(e) and home office integration

[SARS Interpretation Note 47, Issue 5](https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-intr-in-2012-47-wear-and-tear-depreciation-allowance/) states the section 11(e) trade-use depreciation should use the applicable useful-life schedule, prorate both part-year trade use and private-use share, and begin only when brought into trade use. A qualifying standalone small item **costing less than R7,000** may be written off fully; an item costing exactly R7,000, or one piece of a set, does not qualify for that concession. Used assets require a reasonable remaining useful life, not the new-asset table applied mechanically. A personal asset later put to trade use uses the lower of original cost and market value at that point.

[SARS home-office guidance](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/personal-income-tax/filing-season/home-office-expenses/) requires a room equipped, regularly and exclusively used for trade; for salary-dominant employees, more than 50% of duties must occur there. For qualifying premises-linked expenses, **office area / total residence area × qualifying costs**. Equipment wear and tear is a separate calculation, not floor-area apportioned. An employee's mortgage interest is disallowed as a home-office claim from the 2023 assessment year. Example: 20 m² dedicated office / 200 m² home and R12,000 eligible rent/electricity gives R1,200 premises allocation; a desk used for trade is considered under 11(e) separately. A shared dining area or a room also used as a bedroom fails the exclusive-use gate even if the ratio arithmetic can be computed. [SARS's evidence FAQ](https://www.sars.gov.za/faq/what-documents-will-be-required-in-order-to-enable-the-employee-to-submit-their-returns-accurately/) lists employer permission, floor plan, photos, duties schedule, invoices and proof of payment.

For every tool: reject negative amounts, impossible dates, zero or invalid denominators, overlapping deductions, and input years unsupported by the rate schedule. Display year of assessment versus financial year-end clearly, show schedule and assumptions, and call the result an estimate. Eligibility, recoupments, grants, disposal, complex lease rights and prior claims may alter it; an arithmetic calculator should not certify a claim.

## Narrow final-implementation fixtures and gates

The [annexure to SARS Interpretation Note 47, Issue 5](https://www.sars.gov.za/lapd-intr-in-2012-47-wear-and-tear-depreciation-allowance/), pp. 22–26, accepts these **straight-line** useful lives for qualifying assets first used on or after 24 March 2020: personal computer **3 years**; computer tablet/similar device **2 years**; furniture and fittings **6 years**; electronic office equipment **3 years**, mechanical office equipment **5 years**; photocopying equipment **5 years**; cellular telephone **2 years**. A printer should not be silently classified as a photocopier; ask for an established category or block. These selected new-asset classes support a bounded 11(e) calculator. Other categories, used/personal assets later brought into trade, lessor assets, alternative method, shorter-life motivation, prior allowances, disposal and connected-person arrangements need separate review. The same note, pp. 13–18, allows straight-line or diminishing value, requires private/trade and part-year apportionment, and limits the standalone small-item immediate deduction to **cost strictly less than R7,000**, excluding parts of a set and lessor letting assets.

Independent simple fixtures: a qualifying R12,000 new personal computer in full-year, 100% trade use is **R4,000 per year for three years** under straight-line; at 50% confirmed trade use its annual claim component is **R2,000**, not R4,000. A qualifying standalone R6,999 item wholly used for trade can be **R6,999 in its first qualifying year**; an otherwise identical R7,000 personal computer instead follows the three-year straight-line schedule, first full-year amount **R2,333.33** before final rounding reconciliation. An item that is one part of a set is excluded from the small-item concession even at R6,999. A midyear start requires pro-rata use; the note establishes that rule but does not supply a universal month/day convention for every scenario, so state and test the calculator's chosen actual-use period transparently rather than claim an exact statutory day fraction.

For a bounded **11(f)** lease-premium model, add affirmative confirmation that the premium constitutes income for the lessor under the Act. [IN109 §3.2.7, p. 17](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Legal/Notes/Legal-IntR-IN-109-Lease-premiums.pdf) denies the lessee allowance if it does not, subject to a narrow long-term offshore electronic-transmission cable exception outside the model. Also require an actually paid premium, income-producing use, definite documented entitlement including probable renewals, and no early end or complicated recoupment. For a full-month-aligned schedule, [IN109 Example 3](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Legal/Notes/Legal-IntR-IN-109-Lease-premiums.pdf) has R20,000 premium, a two-year entitlement, occupation 1 July, December financial year-end: first six months **R20,000 / 2 × 6/12 = R5,000**. Example 4 has a six-year probable entitlement, R50,000 premium, first six months **R4,167** and the following eight months until early termination **R5,556**, with **no automatic R40,277 balance deduction**. Do not accept partial-month dates into a month-count algorithm as if they were complete months.

For bounded **11(g)** improvements, add affirmative confirmation that the improvement value/right constitutes lessor income. [IN110 §3.2.5, p. 13](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Legal/Notes/Legal-IntR-IN-110-Leasehold-improvements.pdf) excludes a tax-exempt lessor in an ordinary current agreement; historical pre-2 November 2010 exceptions are outside the model. The allowance starts on **completion**, not signature or commencement, and spreads actual cost capped at an established contractual amount or fair reasonable value over the remaining entitlement to use, maximum 25 years. [IN110 Example 1](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Legal/Notes/Legal-IntR-IN-110-Leasehold-improvements.pdf) has cost R550,000, stipulated R600,000, completion on **28 February**, the last day of year 2, and a 20-year lease from 1 March year 1: **zero in year 2**, then **R28,947 per year over the remaining 19 years**. Example 2 has R650,000 spent but R600,000 stipulated, completion 31 August year 2, 18.5 years remaining: **R16,216** for the initial six months, **R32,432** for each following full year, final **R32,440** after rounding. The note says an actual early termination can permit the remaining balance in that year, but a cession is not the same thing; a simplified no-termination schedule should block both rather than invent a write-off.
