# Joff Tax: SARS rule research for individuals

Verified against official primary sources on **7 October 2026 (SAST)**. This is an implementation note for a bounded consumer readiness and estimate release, not a representation that Joff Tax can prepare or submit an ITR12/IRP6 or reproduce a SARS assessment. SARS uses “2026 tax year” for **1 March 2025–28 February 2026** and “2027 tax year” for **1 March 2026–28 February 2027** ([SARS tax years](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-rates/)). Store `assessmentYear` as 2026/2027 and the corresponding start/end dates explicitly; do not confuse a 2026 filing season with 2027 income.

## Status of rules

The 2026 individual table is confirmed by SARS and the gazetted [Rates and Monetary Amounts and Amendment of Revenue Laws Act 3 of 2026, Schedule I](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Legal/AmendActs/Legal-LPrim-AA-2026-01-Rates-and-Monetary-Amounts-and-Amendment-of-Revenue-Laws-Act-3-of-2026-GG-54446-1-April-2026.pdf). Despite its 2026 title and April publication, that Act's Schedule I explicitly applies the R237,100 first bracket to years commencing **on or after 1 March 2025**. Do not mistake it for enactment of the 2027 R245,100 table.

For 2027, [SARS's individual rates page](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-rates/income-tax/rates-of-tax-for-individuals/) and [2027 employer guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/guide-for-employers-in-respect-of-employees-tax-2027/) publish the rates below; the latter says payroll deduction tables took effect **1 March 2026**, while describing Budget measures as “proposals”. National Treasury's [2026 Draft Rates Bill](https://www.treasury.gov.za/legislation/draft_bills/2026%20Draft%20Rates%20Bill%20-%2025%20Feb%202026.pdf) remained a draft in the official material checked; its [4 June 2026 notice](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2026/2026060401%20Media%20statement%20-%20Publication%20of%20the%202026%20Draft%20Rates%20and%20Monetary%20Amounts%20and%20Amendment%20of%20Revenue%20Laws%20Bill.pdf) invited comments to 26 June. Therefore mark 2027 outputs **current SARS-published estimate, subject to legislation and final assessment**, and check for a later enacted Act before claiming final statutory status. The open 2027 assessment is not yet filable.

**Medical scheme fees tax credit (MTC).** A non-refundable reduction of normal tax for qualifying contributions actually paid to a registered medical scheme or similar foreign fund. For each eligible contribution month, first covered person and second covered person receive R364 each in 2026 or R376 each in 2027; every further dependant R246 (2026) or R254 (2027). If payer is not a scheme member, the first two eligible dependants still count as first and second covered persons. Count actual months and eligible people, not an automatic 12 months. Unused MTC is not carried forward ([SARS medical credits](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/personal-income-tax/medical-credits/)). Do not call it a deduction or a cash refund. **Additional medical expenses tax credit (AMTC) can arise from fees alone:** for age 65+ or a qualifying disability of taxpayer/spouse/child, 33.3% of qualifying out-of-pocket expenses **plus fees exceeding 3× annual MTC**; for others, 25% of the positive excess of (qualifying expenses plus fees exceeding 4× MTC) over 7.5% of taxable income excluding specified lump sums. Disability of another dependant does not confer the special 33.3% category. See [SARS AMTC formula](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/personal-income-tax/additional-medical-expenses-tax-credit/). If AMTC is omitted, describe the result specifically as **base estimate excluding additional medical credit**; a complete tax/refund estimate is especially unsafe for 65+ medical members, or stop that path unless all eligible fees/expenses/disability tests are implemented. Extension to some statutory schemes is explicitly a **proposal** in [SARS Budget FAQ](https://www.sars.gov.za/about/sars-tax-and-customs-system/budget/budget-2026-frequently-asked-questions/).

For a bounded **no-disability** calculation, set `M = sum(monthly entitlement for months actually paid)`, `F = qualifying annual scheme fees paid or deemed paid by the taxpayer`, `E = qualifying other medical expenses actually paid and borne, not recoverable from any fund/insurer`, and `T = taxable income after allowable section 11F deduction` (no excluded lump sums in the narrow path). Then `AMTC_65plus = 0.333 × (max(0, F − 3M) + E)` and `AMTC_under65 = 0.25 × max(0, max(0, F − 4M) + E − 0.075T)`. Use age on the assessment's last day for the 65+ category ([SARS medical credits guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Ops/Guides/Legal-Pub-Guide-IT07-Guide-on-the-Determination-of-Medical-Tax-Credits.pdf)); both credits reduce tax only down to zero. The [SARS AMTC page](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/personal-income-tax/additional-medical-expenses-tax-credit/) names qualifying practitioners, hospital/nursing, prescribed medicines, comparable foreign expenditure and certain prescribed disability/impairment expenditure. Do not count medical claims refunded by a scheme or unqualified wellness spending. Ask the user to confirm `E = 0` rather than assume it. Employer-paid medical contributions are a taxable benefit but deemed paid by the employee for credit purposes ([SARS FAQ](https://www.sars.gov.za/faq/faq-what-is-deemed-medical-contributions/)); reconcile source code **4005**, don't add the employer amount a second time ([SARS FAQ on IRP5](https://www.sars.gov.za/faq/faq-which-amount-do-i-capture-for-medical-scheme-contributions-made-by-my-employer/)). More than one person paying fees for the same covered person requires apportioning MTC in the fee-payment ratio ([SARS 2027 employer guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/guide-for-employers-in-respect-of-employees-tax-2027/)); exclude from simple input path. Disability cases require separate validation and should hand off.

### Independently computed bounded examples

These are hand-checkable examples of the above formulas using SARS rates, not SARS-issued assessments. All people are SA residents with a single ordinary salary employer, no foreign income, other income, allowances, fringe benefits, prior carryovers, CGT, disability, lump sums, other deductions or shared medical payments. In the two 2026 examples the taxpayer personally pays RA and medical fees, so the salary is the **annual taxable remuneration before section 11F**, not take-home pay; the RA contribution is not already removed. Medical E is paid and unrecoverable qualifying expense only. Monetary results retain calculation precision, with display rounding to cents; SARS assessment rounding may differ.

| Example | Inputs and calculation | Indicative tax R |
|---|---|---:|
| **2026, age 40, no AMTC** | Pre-11F salary 300,000; RA 30,000, allowed `min(30,000, 350,000, 27.5%×300,000, 300,000)=30,000`; `T=270,000`. One medical member for 12 paid months: `M=12×364=4,368`; fees `F=24,000`, explicitly `E=0`. Under-65 excess `max(0, 24,000−4×4,368)+0−7.5%×270,000 = 6,528−20,250 < 0`, so AMTC 0. Bracket tax `42,678+26%×(270,000−237,100)=51,232`; less primary rebate 17,235 and MTC 4,368. | **29,629.00** |
| **2026, age 40, positive AMTC** | Same salary/RA/T and `M=4,368`; fees `F=36,000` and eligible paid, unrecoverable expense `E=20,000`. AMTC base `(36,000−17,472)+20,000−20,250=18,278`; AMTC `25%×18,278=4,569.50`. Bracket tax 51,232 less rebate 17,235, MTC 4,368 and AMTC 4,569.50. | **25,059.50** |
| **2027, age 68, fees-only AMTC** | Pre-11F salary `T=400,000`; no retirement deduction. One medical member for 12 paid months: `M=12×376=4,512`; fees `F=24,000`, explicitly `E=0`. AMTC `33.3%×(24,000−3×4,512)=0.333×10,464=3,484.512`. Bracket tax `79,998+31%×(400,000−383,100)=85,237`; age rebates `17,820+9,765=27,585`; less MTC and AMTC. | **49,655.49** |
| **2027, age 77, zero liability** | Ordinary salary `T=80,000`; no RA, scheme fees or E, so MTC/AMTC zero. Bracket tax `18%×80,000=14,400`; age rebates `17,820+9,765+3,249=30,834`; floor at zero. No cash payment arises from unused rebates. | **0.00** |

The 2027 examples use SARS's published current rates, with the draft-legislation caveat above. At annual contribution/member-count boundaries, ask for each paid month's coverage; do not multiply the year-end family size by 12. For the standard under-65 AMTC, calculate the **fee excess before** the 7.5% test, as shown in the second row. For age 65+, E is creditable without that 7.5% threshold, as shown by the third row's fees-only result.

**Retirement fund contributions.** For 2026 the section 11F annual cap is R350,000. SARS's 2025/26 guide describes the deduction as the lesser of actual qualifying pension/provident/RA contributions (including employer contributions treated as employee fringe benefit), 27.5% of greater qualifying remuneration or taxable income, R350,000, and a further limit based on taxable income before taxable capital gains. Excess may carry forward with adjustments ([2025/26 SARS guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/20256/Budget-tax-guide-21-May-2025.pdf)). SARS's [2027 employer guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/guide-for-employers-in-respect-of-employees-tax-2027/) uses R430,000 with the same 27.5%/taxable-income limitations; the change from R350,000 to R430,000 appears in the [2026 **Draft** Rates Bill, clause 7](https://www.treasury.gov.za/legislation/draft_bills/2026%20Draft%20Rates%20Bill%20-%2025%20Feb%202026.pdf). Thus use R430,000 only for a labeled 2027 estimate pending enacted verification. A blanket `min(contributions, 0.275 * salary, cap)` will be wrong with capital gains, other taxable income, fringe benefits or carry-forwards; either gather all inputs or keep this outside the first numerical scope.

For a verified **single ordinary salary, no-other-income, no-CGT, no-other-deductions, no-prior-carryover** case, the eligible current-year section 11F deduction can reduce to `min(totalEligibleCurrentContributions, 27.5% × qualifyingBefore11FRemuneration, qualifyingBefore11FTaxableIncomeExcludingCGT, annualCap)`. These two income bases should agree only if the employment/benefit inputs reconcile; do not silently substitute take-home pay or already-deducted taxable income. SARS expressly says that with no other income remuneration is used ([SARS FAQ](https://www.sars.gov.za/faq/faq-what-is-the-impact-of-section-11k-on-me-as-a-taxpayer/)), but the full three-limit formula remains relevant ([SARS section 11F note](https://www.sars.gov.za/latest-news/retirement-fund-contribution-deductions-section-11f2a/)). Employer contributions are taxable fringe benefits and deemed employee contributions. On an IRP5, **4001 pension, 4003 provident, 4006 RA** identify the total current contributions (including relevant arrears), while **4029** is a return-level retirement-deduction code and **4018 is old income-protection contributions**, not a retirement amount ([SARS ITR12 guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Ops/Guides/IT-AE-36-G05-Comprehensive-Guide-to-the-ITR12-Income-Tax-Return-for-Individuals-External-Guide.pdf); [source-code directory](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/personal-income-tax/filing-season/find-a-source-code/)). Do not add employer fringe-benefit contributions again when already included in totals, and do not subtract prepopulated/deducted amounts a second time. The ITR12 guide describes SARS as calculating allowable deduction and carrying excess. If contribution totals, fringe benefits or pre-deduction basis cannot be reconciled from the IRP5, switch to preparation-only.

**South African-source interest exemption.** Both years: R23,800 annually for a person **under 65** and R34,500 for **65 or older**. Apply to local-source interest, not foreign interest, and not interest inside a tax-free investment (separately exempt) ([SARS interest table](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-rates/income-tax/interest-and-dividends/); [SARS 2026/27 FAQ](https://www.sars.gov.za/about/sars-tax-and-customs-system/budget/budget-2026-frequently-asked-questions/)). In a simple estimate, include `max(0, annualLocalInterest − exemption)` in taxable income; verify residency and income types first. No blanket exemption for investment income generally.

## Dates, filing and provisional tax

The officially published **2026 assessment ITR12 filing season** is: auto-assessment notices 1–12 July 2026; non-provisional individuals not auto-assessed can submit 13 July–**23 October 2026**; provisional individuals not auto-assessed 13 July 2026–**22 January 2027**. A taxpayer who agrees with a SARS auto-assessment has no further action; a taxpayer who disagrees may correct/submit a return, and eligible provisional taxpayers may themselves be auto-assessed. Do not tell every user they must file. [SARS Filing Season](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/personal-income-tax/filing-season/) and [Changes for Filing Season 2026](https://www.sars.gov.za/latest-news/changes-for-filing-season-2026/). **No 2027 assessment ITR12 filing-season dates were officially published in the reviewed material by 7 October 2026**; represent them as `unknown`, never copy the 2026 dates forward.

The IRP6 estimated-income **provisional payment schedule** is different from the ITR12 season. [SARS provisional tax](https://www.sars.gov.za/types-of-tax/provisional-tax/) says first payment within six months of start (normally 31 August), second by last working day of assessment year (last business day of February), and optional third/top-up by last business day of September for individuals. [SARS FAQ](https://www.sars.gov.za/faq/faq-when-must-provisional-tax-be-paid/) says when nominal date is a weekend/public holiday, payment is due the **last business day before**. SARS's 2026 February digest explicitly advised paying the 28 February 2026 Saturday obligation by **Friday 27 February 2026** ([digest](https://www.sars.gov.za/monthly-digest/monthly-tax-digest-february-2026/)). Do not hardcode a 28 February 2027 due date just because it is the end of the 2027 assessment; it falls on a Sunday, so apply official last-business-day logic, and verify published SARS calendar before user reminders. The SARS [2027 provisional guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/guide-to-provisional-tax/) currently contains stale/inconsistent dates and an impossible “29 February 2027”; prefer the general rule plus a specifically confirmed official calendar date. Do not show 2026 ITR12 deadline as an IRP6 deadline.

Provisional status is more nuanced than “freelancer or rental income”: [SARS 2026/27 pocket guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/wp-content/uploads/Docs/Budget/Budget2026/Budget-tax-guide-2026-online-version-for-printing.pdf) defines a person earning non-remuneration income or remuneration from an unregistered employer, subject to exemptions where an individual carries on no business and taxable income is below the applicable tax threshold, or specified interest/dividend/foreign dividend/rental/unregistered-employer remuneration does not exceed R30,000. Business activity defeats that simple exemption. Distinguish a **possible provisional taxpayer** prompt from a firm classification, and route complex cases to SARS/practitioner.

## Submission, professional status and data protection

SARS has an **Independent Software Vendor interface** for ITR12 and IRP6 return submission, but it is a controlled interface requiring vendor authorization, signed terms, a **product/application-specific access key**, authenticated active eFiling user with appropriate rights ([SARS ISV page](https://www.sars.gov.za/individuals/i-need-help-with-my-tax/your-tax-questions-answered/independent-software-vendors/)). It is not evidence of a public anonymous API. Bulk third-party data HTTPS/Connect:Direct channels cover enumerated IT3, INS, MED, TRN, FTI and CRS data and require enrolment/certificates/declarations; they are **not ITR12 submission endpoints** ([SARS HTTPS guide](https://www.sars.gov.za/guide-for-the-submission-of-third-party-data-using-the-https-channel/)). Initial release should deep-link to official eFiling/MobiApp and never request/store users' SARS password/OTP or claim direct SARS integration.

If a human provides tax-related services to taxpayers as a tax practitioner, SARS says the individual must register with both an approved Recognised Controlling Body and SARS, obtain a PR number and activate tax-practitioner status on eFiling ([SARS registration process](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-practitioners/tax-practitioner-registration-process/); [controlling bodies](https://www.sars.gov.za/tax-practitioners/controlling-bodies-for-tax-practitioners/)). Product copy should not imply Joff Tax or its staff are a registered practitioner without actual registration; the line between an automated estimate and regulated tax service should be reviewed before offering individualized advice or filing on behalf of people.

The [Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013](https://inforegulator.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/InfoRegSA-act-2013-004.pdf) applies to tax/identity/health data processing. Operational requirements to design for: section 10 minimality; section 11 lawful basis (consent is one route, not the only route); sections 13–14 specific purpose and retention; section 18 notice at collection (responsible party, purposes, source, recipients, cross-border intention and rights); sections 19–21 risk-appropriate security and written operator safeguards; section 22 security-compromise notifications; sections 23–24 access/correction; sections 26–27 extra conditions for special personal information (medical/disability data), and section 72 conditions before transfer to a foreign third party. The [Information Regulator POPIA guidance](https://inforegulator.org.za/popia/) says all security compromises are reportable without a risk threshold and responsible parties should notify the Regulator and affected subjects as soon as reasonably possible; its [Information Officer portal guidance](https://inforegulator.org.za/portal-posts/) says public/private bodies register an Information Officer. Make an explicit data map and lawful-basis/privacy notice, minimize saved health/ID documents and access, define retention/deletion and incident process, assess cloud/AI subprocessors and cross-border flow, and register the responsible party's Information Officer as applicable. A local-only calculator avoids much sensitive collection. This is product risk guidance, not a completed legal compliance assessment.

## Suggested first-release behavior and limitations

1. A **2026 filing readiness checklist** asks whether SARS auto-assessed, provides the correct published ITR12 date for the user's confirmed status, gathers IRP5, local-interest, retirement and medical certificates, and links to official SARS eFiling. It does not infer that everyone above/below a threshold must submit.
2. A **2026/2027 plain-income estimator** can show marginal tax after age rebate, a correctly reconciled current-year section 11F deduction, MTC and AMTC when all their bounded inputs are known, with year-specific data and the 2027 status label. Calculate normal tax from `max(0, before11FIncome − allowable11F)`; then apply rebates and credits without a negative tax. If eligible expenses cannot be established, label it `estimated base tax excluding AMTC` and withhold a definitive PAYE/refund comparison. An optional PAYE comparison is not a SARS refund promise: assessment, prepopulated third-party data, other credits and prior balances can differ.
3. Block or hand off cases with foreign residency/income/dividends, capital gains, rental/business income or assessed losses, travel/home-office allowances, multiple employers, tax directives, two-pot and other lump sums/severance, retirement carryovers/employer fringe-benefit mismatch, disability/additional medical claims, donations, provisional IRP6 estimates, or prior SARS adjustments. Individualized advice and actual ITR12 submission require separate product/legal/integration work.
4. Version every numeric rule by assessment year and source URL/retrieval date. Snapshot sources before each filing season and test bracket boundaries, age on the final assessment day, covered-member-month counts and zero-floor credits. Show a rule-review date in the UI; stop 2027 filing-deadline UI until SARS publishes its season.

Note: SARS's dynamic guides and FAQ occasionally contain stale year labels or inconsistent provisional dates; the quoted assessment periods, published 2026 filing-season notice, current tax tables and Act/draft status govern this bounded product configuration. Recheck material before launch and after any legislative changes.
