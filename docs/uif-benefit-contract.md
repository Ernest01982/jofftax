# UIF contribution and benefit estimate contract

Reviewed 7 October 2026. This is a bounded public planning calculator, not a UIF claim decision, credit ledger, live status or guaranteed payment. Its benefit arithmetic comes from the Department of Employment and Labour, not TaxTim's published formula. [TaxTim's current UIF page](https://www.taxtim.com/za/calculators/uif) offers contributions and unemployment, illness and maternity estimates, but says credits accrue at one day per **five** worked days. [The Department's calculation guide](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Publications/Unemployment%20Insurance%20Fund/EASY%20AID%20Guide%20Spreadsheet%20Application.pdf), [2022 rights guide](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Publications/Unemployment%20Insurance%20Fund/KNOW%20YOUR%20UIF%20RIGHTS%202022.pdf) and [2025 UIF presentation](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Publications/Occupational%20Health%20and%20Safety/OHS%20Conference%202025%20-/Day%201%20Presentations/UIF%20Compliance%20Presentation%2026022025.pdf) consistently say **one benefit credit day per four completed days of contributing employment**, subject to 365 in a four-year cycle. Use the official 1:4 rule. This discrepancy is material: four years of continuous contributing work can reach 365 credits, rather than only about 292 under TaxTim's example.

## Contributions

For a covered employee's monthly remuneration, employee contribution = employer contribution = 1% × min(nonnegative monthly remuneration, R17,712), max R177.12 each per month. Total = twice that. The [Department's 2025 presentation](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Publications/Occupational%20Health%20and%20Safety/OHS%20Conference%202025%20-/Day%201%20Presentations/UIF%20Compliance%20Presentation%2026022025.pdf) confirms the 1% shares and R17,712 ceiling. A contribution calculator does not decide whether a particular worker is covered or entitled to benefits.

## Bounded unemployment benefit estimate

For a straightforward contributor with **no prior UIF benefits claimed in the relevant four-year cycle** and known actual completed contributing days:

1. Ask for the average gross monthly remuneration over the preceding six months (if fluctuating), completed contributing employment days during the four-year lookback, requested unemployment days, and confirmation that no prior benefits were paid in that cycle. Do not infer exact credits from a casual “months worked” guess.
2. Let `Y = min(monthly remuneration, R17,712) × 12 / 365` Rand daily. The percentage `IRR = clamp(29.2 + 7173.92 / (232.92 + Y), 38, 60)`. Daily first-tier benefit = `Y × IRR / 100`; late-tier daily benefit = `Y × 0.20`.
3. Available credit days `C = min(365, floor(completed contributing days / 4))`. Benefit days in this illustration `D = min(requested days, C)`. First-tier days `H = min(D, 238)`; tail days `T = max(0, D - 238)`. Illustrative total `H × Y × IRR/100 + T × Y × 0.20`. Show the cap and both tiers. Round currency for display, not on each intermediate step.
4. If prior claims, uncertain contributing history, partial unemployment, unusual declared earnings or eligibility uncertainty applies, show the daily rate and a named reason why a total cannot be estimated reliably from these inputs. The Department decides the actual credit balance and eligible duration.

Known-answer fixture: R17,712 average monthly pay, 1,460 completed contributing days, no prior claim, 365 requested days yields `Y ≈ R582.3123`, 38% IRR, 365 available credits, 238 first-tier days and 127 late-tier days. Unrounded aggregate ≈ **R67,455.06**. For 400 contributing days, only 100 credits can be estimated; 365 requested days must not turn into 365 paid days. 3 completed days yield zero credits. The actual period of unemployment may shorten payment.

The [2018 Labour Laws Amendment Act](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201811/42062gon1305act10of2018.pdf) changed benefit accrual from 1:5 to 1:4. The [2016 amendment](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Acts/UIF/Unemployment%20Insurance%20Amendment%20Act%202016.pdf) had earlier changed the old 238-day cap to 365. Official [government unemployment guidance](https://www.gov.za/services/services-residents/world-work/uif/uif-unemployment-benefits) says voluntary resignation usually does not qualify, with constructive dismissal an exception; claim eligibility must be separately determined. Do not present the monetary estimate as entitlement.

## Illness and maternity component estimates: statutory correction

**Correction recorded 7 October 2026 before illness implementation.** A [Department 2022 rights guide](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Publications/Unemployment%20Insurance%20Fund/KNOW%20YOUR%20UIF%20RIGHTS%202022.pdf) and its [2025 I-DEAL infographic](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Publications/Newsletter-%20I-%20DEAL/2025%20Newsletter%20I-DEAL/IDEAL%205_2025.pdf) visually group illness with maternity at 66%. That conflicts with the binding amended [Unemployment Insurance Act](https://www.gov.za/documents/unemployment-insurance-act): section 12(3)(c), inserted by the [2016 amendment](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Acts/UIF/Unemployment%20Insurance%20Amendment%20Act%202016.pdf), expressly gives 66% **for Part D maternity**. Illness is Part C. Sections 12(3)(b) and (d) prescribe the income-replacement scale for ordinary benefits, and section 21(2) limits illness benefit plus paid sick leave to normal remuneration. The [UIF 2021/22 annual report](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Reports/Annual%20Reports/Unemployment%20Insurance%20Fund/2021/UIF%20ANNUAL%20REPORT%202021%202022.pdf) independently describes the IRR as minimum 38%, with **66% for maternity**, for the first 238 days and 20% for the remaining 127. The 2025 infographic's illness grouping must not override the Act. The prior unqualified 66% illness proposal is withdrawn.

For **maternity**, a disclosed normal monthly salary `S` and actual monthly leave pay `L` give an illustrative daily component `min(0.66 × min(S,R17,712) × 12/365, max(0,(S-L) × 12/365))`, subject to the separate maternity eligibility/credit rules. The second term uses uncapped normal pay minus leave pay. Example: normal R30,000, leave pay R25,000, capped 66% daily amount ≈ R384.33 and wage shortfall ≈ R164.38/day; bounded maternity top-up is **R164.38/day**. Full normal paid leave gives zero.

For **illness**, use the ordinary IRR instead of the maternity 66% rate. Set `Y = min(S,R17,712) × 12/365`, then `firstTierDaily = min(Y × IRR(Y), max(0,(S-L) × 12/365))` for the first **238 credited days** and `laterTierDaily = min(0.20 × Y, max(0,(S-L) × 12/365))` for days **239–365**, subject to confirmed eligibility, available credit days and actual period of illness. The wage-shortfall cap uses uncapped normal monthly salary `S` and actual leave pay `L`; it does not increase a 20% or IRR benefit to replace all lost wages. For normal R30,000 and leave pay R25,000, the 38% capped daily amount ≈ R221.28 exceeds the R164.38/day wage shortfall, so first-tier top-up is **R164.38/day**; the later-tier amount is ≈ **R116.46/day**. That first example masks the legal difference from maternity because its shortfall cap binds. At R30,000 normal and **zero** leave pay, first-tier illness is ≈ **R221.28/day**, whereas maternity is ≈ R384.33/day. Without complete four-year contribution/claim history and available credit days, show only clearly labelled first/late daily components and an explicit blocker for an aggregate amount. [The official UIF annual report](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Reports/Annual%20Reports/Unemployment%20Insurance%20Fund/2021/UIF%20ANNUAL%20REPORT%202021%202022.pdf) independently states the 238-day IRR and remaining 127-day 20% scale with maternity as the 66% exception.

[The Department's maternity statement](https://www.labour.gov.za/Media-Desk/Media-Statements/Pages/UIF-embarks-on-visits-to-local-hospitals-in-North-West-Province-to-assist-pregnant-women-with-maternity-benefit-application.aspx) describes a **maximum 121 maternity days**, subject to eligibility and sufficient credit days, and at least 13 weeks employment before application. [The 2016 amendment](https://www.labour.gov.za/DocumentCenter/Acts/UIF/Unemployment%20Insurance%20Amendment%20Act%202016.pdf) reduces the minimum illness period to seven days; [government illness guidance](https://www.gov.za/services/services-residents/world-work/uif/illness-benefits-uif) says the contributor must receive less than normal wages. Use these as screening prompts. Maternity is de-linked from unemployment credits in a way that depends on claim sequence and history; prior claims or unknown balance should block an aggregate estimate and show an official UIF next step. For illness, medical certification, covered leave, actual credit balance and other earnings matter. A daily component may be shown without pretending the full payout is determined.

The Department's [uFiling landing page](https://ufiling.labour.gov.za/uif/) currently says employee benefit claims are **no longer processed there** and directs claimants to `uifonline.labour.gov.za`. Link to that official landing page for the current route rather than hard-coding an obsolete application path. The old rights guide still mentions uFiling for claims, so current portal instructions take precedence.

## Presentation and tests

- Explicit modes: monthly contributions; unemployment; illness; maternity. Each result names its own rule, inputs, scope and official source. A user must choose a mode rather than seeing contribution arithmetic masquerading as all UIF coverage.
- Validate nonnegative finite remuneration and leave pay, integer nonnegative completed contributing/claim days, and `leavePay <= normalPay`. Explain rather than manufacture a benefit if history or eligibility is unknown.
- Show both employee and employer contribution; for R25,000 monthly covered pay, each is R177.12, not R250.
- Unemployment at R17,712 and 1,460 completed days yields the fixture above. At 400 days, cap claim to 100 credits. The 239th day uses 20% rather than IRR.
- Illness/maternity full-pay leave results in zero illustrative top-up. Illness must not reuse the maternity 66% rate. Maternity requests beyond 121 days are capped or blocked with explanation.
- Do not state that UIF benefits will be paid, that the user qualifies, or that Joff is connected to the Department. The official claim and declared credit record control.
